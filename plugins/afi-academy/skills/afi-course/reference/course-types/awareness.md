# Course type: `awareness`

**Pick it when** the learner must notice, recognise and report, rather than perform a procedure: heat illness signs, a
yearly refresher, spotting a hazard and telling someone.

## Defaults

| Length | Lessons | Test | Pass mark | Certificate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| about 15 min | 2–3 | pool 10, draw 5 | 80% (**asked**: offer it as the recommended answer) | 12 months |

Other defaults (mark them `default`):
- `practice`: the kit's rhythm rule for the audience (floor or office), plus the final test.
- `difficulty`: applied questions (a situation to judge), not definitions.
- `contentKinds`: photo, drawn, voiceover, jobaid; which picture goes where follows the kit's imagery rule (writing
  guide, Pictures).
- `handsOn` (asked when safety-critical; this is the recommended answer): none; supervisors watch for the signs on the
  floor (`supervisorCheck`).
- `jobAid`: a one-page card ("signs to watch for, who to call").
- `pilotRun` (if safety-critical): 3–5 real learners before publish.
- `reviewAgain`: 12 months, or when the guidance changes.

## Structure

The kit's writing guide, **Recipes → Annual refresher (`awareness`)**. Short and story-led: the real case drives the
story and the scenario.

## Fields that apply

All of `brief-fields.md`, plus:
- **Asked:** one real case (`realCase`); when there is a test, the pass mark and must-pass questions; when
  safety-critical, the hands-on sign-off and the limits; "Has the expert seen this brief?" is asked with the brief
  (`brief-fields.md` § Approval).
- `jobAid`, and `pilotRun` when safety-critical.

## Research starting points

- osha.gov: the topic page and its guidance (for example the heat page, `https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure`).
- ecfr.gov, Title 29: check whether a specific standard applies. If only guidance exists, say so in the brief and cite
  the guidance; never present guidance as a legal requirement.
- NIOSH / CDC pages for signs, symptoms and first steps (public domain).
- AFI's own policy or practice: who to call, where help is, local rules. Ask the author for it.
