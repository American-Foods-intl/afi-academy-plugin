# Course type: `safety_skill`

**Pick it when** the learner must perform a physical procedure correctly and a mistake can hurt someone: a
pallet-jack check before use, using a fire extinguisher, lockout.

## Defaults

| Length | Lessons | Test | Pass mark | Certificate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20–30 min | 3 | pool 20, draw 10, with must-pass items | 80% (**asked**: offer it as the recommended answer) | 12 months |

Other defaults (mark them `default`):
- `practice`: the kit's rhythm rule for floor courses, plus the final test.
- `difficulty`: applied questions (a real label, a floor situation), not definitions.
- `contentKinds`: photo, official, drawn, voiceover, jobaid; which picture goes where follows the kit's imagery rule
  (writing guide, Pictures).
- `handsOn` (asked when safety-critical; this is the recommended answer): a supervisor watches each person do the
  procedure and records the sign-off in the Academy. Say so when the standard requires an observed evaluation.
- `jobAid`: a printable one-page card for the floor.
- `pilotRun` (if safety-critical): 3–5 real learners from the floor before publish.
- `reviewAgain`: 12 months, or when the regulation, the equipment or the chemicals change.

## Structure

The kit's writing guide, **Recipes → Safety skill (`safety_skill`, `floor`)**. Plan the lessons on that recipe; the
brief lists one line per lesson, not a storyboard.

## Fields that apply

All of `brief-fields.md`, plus:
- **Asked:** one real case (`realCase`); and, when safety-critical (usually): the hands-on sign-off and the limits.
  "Has the expert seen this brief?" is asked with the brief (`brief-fields.md` § Approval).
- `mustPass`: the questions where a wrong answer could hurt someone.
- `handsOn`: flag it when the regulation requires an observed evaluation (read the standard's training paragraph).
- `jobAid`, `pilotRun`.

## Research starting points

- The standard on ecfr.gov, Title 29: `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.<number>`. Read its scope,
  requirements and training paragraphs, and cite each requirement to the paragraph. Note the "up to date as of" date.
  Common ones: 1910.1200 (hazard communication), 1910.178 (powered industrial trucks), 1910.132 (PPE), 1910.147
  (lockout), 1910.151 (first aid, eyewash).
- osha.gov: the topic page, quick cards and fact sheets (public domain; official images such as pictograms come from
  here).
- AFI's own written program or SOP for the site: how AFI does it (where things are kept, who to call). Ask for it if
  the author hasn't supplied it.
- The equipment or product manual: usually background, unless the author says it is the final word. If it disagrees
  with the SOP or the standard, follow the conflict rule in `intake.md`.
